Two sessions clear the vigorous week by one minute

The prescription everyone quotes is 150 moderate minutes a week. There is a second line under it: 75 vigorous minutes does the same job. The 4x4 protocol is the shortest honest way across that line. Ten minutes easy, then four climbs of four minutes near the top of the heart-rate range with four easy minutes between, and the last recovery is the cooldown. One session is 38 minutes on the clock, 16 of them hard, 42 percent of the time you spent.

Two sessions a week is 76 minutes. The vigorous line is 75. The block clears it by one minute, and nothing else in the week owes a minute more. That is the entire cardio requirement met by 92 minutes of calendar time, because the sessions are 38 each and the showers are free.

The numbers need a max heart rate to be worth anything. On 190, the hard windows sit between 85 and 95 percent, which is 161.5 to 180.5, so aim 162 to 180 and stay inside it. Recoveries float near 70 percent, 133. A chest strap beats a watch for this, because the climbs are four minutes and a lagging readout eats two of them.

Measuring the payoff costs either nothing or $129. The watch estimate is free and drifts high on exactly the people who train hardest. A lab test with the gas mask runs $129 a session, so spring and fall is $258 a year. Spread across the 76 structured minutes times 52 weeks, 3,952 minutes, that is six and a half cents a minute to know the ceiling moved.
